Moračnik Monastery stands on the island of the same name near the south-western shore of Lake Skadar. The complex occupies the south-eastern part of the island beside the lake and is dedicated to the Three-Handed Icon of the Most Holy Theotokos.

Moračnik was first mentioned in a 1417 charter of Balša III Đurđević, ruler of Zeta, granting the monastery a salt threshing floor in Bar. The same document shows that it was a dependency of Prečista Krajinska, the important medieval spiritual centre where the Metropolitan of Zeta then resided. It belongs to the group of Lake Skadar holy places that formed a strong monastic centre in the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries known as the Holy Mountain of Zeta.

Monastic life continued after the Ottoman conquest. In the early seventeenth century, monasteries with monastic communities were still recorded on the islands of Lake Skadar; later in the century, Nikolaj is mentioned as abbot of Moračnik. Historical evidence of an active monastery becomes sparse thereafter, and it is thought to have been finally abandoned during the troubled eighteenth century.

The complex is an important example of architecture from the Balšić period. Its main church is a domed triconch building accompanied by a chapel, residence and tall stone tower. Despite centuries of decay, it retains the recognisable enclosed form of a medieval monastery.

Archaeological research and initial conservation began in the second half of the twentieth century. Systematic spiritual and architectural renewal began in 1997, when services resumed and monastic life was gradually restored.

Today the complex comprises the Church of the Three-Handed Icon of the Most Holy Theotokos with a chapel of Saint John of Damascus, a chapel of Saint Gregory of Sinai, the monastery residence and associated buildings. The Church of the Transfiguration also stands on the island’s highest point, several hundred metres from the main complex.
