The Monastery of Saint Basil of Ostrog—Ribnjak stands above Bar, on a plateau beneath Gradac hill. The present complex developed on the site of an older holy place whose beginnings are associated with the Middle Ages and Queen Helen of Anjou.

Foundations of the old Church of Saint John the Baptist survive at Ribnjak. Its construction is placed in the second half of the thirteenth century, most probably the 1280s. It was an elongated single-nave stone church with a rectangular apse, gabled roof and bell gable. Tradition also associates Queen Helen with the Church of Saint Elijah on nearby Volujica.

Events surrounding Bar’s liberation from the Ottoman Empire hold a special place in Ribnjak’s history. The main Montenegrin army camped here in November 1877. According to tradition, Prince Nikola Petrović-Njegoš saw Saint Basil of Ostrog on Gradac hill and was told that Bar would be liberated. After the Ottoman garrison surrendered on 9 January 1878, Prince Nikola decided in gratitude to build a church on Gradac dedicated to Saint Basil.

The Church of Saint Basil was consecrated on 12 May 1879, the saint’s feast. Metropolitan Ilarion Roganović performed the consecration in the presence of Prince Nikola and a large gathering. The assembly at Ribnjak on Saint Basil’s Day then became a tradition in the Bar region.

The modern restoration began in the early 1990s. Construction of a residence started in 1991, and the following year Ribnjak was linked with Ostrog Monastery with Metropolitan Amfilohije’s blessing. In 1993 a new church began to rise on the plateau where the medieval Church of Saint John the Baptist had stood, gradually forming today’s complex.

The old Church of Saint Basil on Gradac was later restored and reconsecrated in 2012. A Church of the Holy Apostles Peter and Paul was also built as the monastery developed: its foundations were consecrated in 2015 and the completed church in 2019.

Today Ribnjak includes the Church of Saint Basil of Ostrog, the restored old church on Gradac, the chapel-baptistery of Saint John the Baptist, the Church of the Holy Apostles Peter and Paul, residences and associated buildings. Remains of a medieval holy place, the memory of Bar’s liberation and the modern renewal of monastic life meet here.
