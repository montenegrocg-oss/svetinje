The Monastery of the Holy Trinity—Stanjevići stands at Pobori in the hinterland of Budva, on the slopes of Lovćen. Its history spans almost seven centuries, and at different times it was an important spiritual and popular centre of Montenegro and the Littoral.

According to preserved tradition, Duke Nikola Stanjević, a prominent nobleman in Emperor Dušan’s time, founded the monastery in the first half of the fourteenth century. Duke Đurđe Đurašev Crnojević was another important benefactor: he built a fortification and Church of Saint George on the hill above and maintained a court at Stanjevići.

The monastery was abandoned in the late fifteenth and early sixteenth centuries as the Ottomans advanced. In the seventeenth century it is mentioned as a dependency of Saint Petka’s Monastery on the Budva Plain. A new era began in the early eighteenth century, especially when Metropolitan Danilo Petrović-Njegoš moved to Stanjevići in 1714 after Cetinje Monastery was destroyed. During several years here, he restored the residence and began building the monastery church.

Metropolitan Sava Petrović-Njegoš continued the renewal and consecrated the Holy Trinity Church in 1736. Stanjevići underwent a notable spiritual and cultural flowering in the mid-eighteenth century. Dositej Obradović visited and was ordained a hieromonk here in 1764. Following another destruction, the monastery was restored again in 1770, and a school opened to teach literacy and prepare future priests.

Stanjevići holds an especially important place in the life of Saint Peter of Cetinje. He lived here for extended periods in the late eighteenth and early nineteenth centuries and sent many of his epistles from the monastery. In 1798, together with representatives of the people, he shaped the first part of the *Stega*, one of the most important early legal acts of modern Montenegro, at Stanjevići.

Petar II Petrović-Njegoš also stayed frequently. In his time, Stanjevići was a meeting place for prominent figures, including Vuk Karadžić. The creation of Njegoš’s poem *The Ray of the Microcosm* is also associated with the monastery.

In the late 1830s the monastery was ceded to Austria and converted into a frontier fort. The complex was destroyed in the 1869 Bay of Kotor uprising, although the Holy Trinity Church remained its best-preserved part. Stanjevići lay in ruins for more than a century, with further damage caused by the 1979 earthquake.

Modern restoration began in 1994. The church, residences and other parts were gradually reconstructed from old descriptions and surviving drawings, alongside the return of monastic and liturgical life. The major renewal ended in 2018, when the restored monastery was solemnly consecrated on its 680th anniversary.

Today the complex comprises the Holy Trinity Church, residences and associated buildings, while the restored Church of Saint George stands at nearby Đurđevac. Its monumental architecture and exceptionally rich history testify to Stanjevići’s importance in Montenegro’s spiritual, cultural and social life.
