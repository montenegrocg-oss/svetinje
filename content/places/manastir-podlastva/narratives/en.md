Podlastva Monastery stands on the southern slope of Lastva Grbaljska, from which it takes its name. Set at the edge of Mrčevo Polje, it was for centuries one of the principal gathering places of the people of Grbalj. Popular assemblies were held here and important religious, political and social decisions made.

Archaeological research in 1984 confirmed the antiquity of Christian life on the site. Part of a mosaic floor and a fragment of a stone parapet slab belonging to an early Christian basilica of the fifth or sixth century were found beneath the present church. Remains of an old baptistery dated to the seventh century lie between the church and residence.

According to folk tradition, Emperor Dušan restored the monastery in 1350. A tradition that Saint Sava stayed in the area before leaving for Mount Athos is also connected with Podlastva. The earliest written mention dates from 1419, while the adoption of the Grbalj Code at Podlastva is associated with 1427.

Because of its importance to Grbalj, the monastery frequently suffered during wars and uprisings. According to the Metropolitanate, the Venetians destroyed it in 1452 after the Grbalj revolt. It was rebuilt around 1700, but suffered again under Austrian and French rule. Following one destruction, it was restored in 1812.

Austrian forces burned the monastery again during the 1869 Bay of Kotor uprising. Restoration was completed in 1874. It suffered another serious destruction in 1915 during the First World War. The church, residences and other buildings were restored between the wars, and Metropolitan Gavrilo Dožić, later Serbian Patriarch, consecrated the renewed complex in 1936.

The monastery was damaged during the Second World War and suffered its worst modern destruction in the earthquake of 15 April 1979. Parts of the church, bell tower and vault collapsed, and the residences were badly damaged. A long restoration followed.

Fragments of fifteenth-century frescoes survive in the church. The building has an elongated plan about 12.5 metres long and four metres wide, with a three-part bell gable. Repeated rebuilding and extensions have left traces of different periods in its long history.

Podlastva’s major modern restoration culminated in 2019. During celebrations of an important monastery anniversary, Serbian Patriarch Irinej consecrated the renewed complex, residences and chapel of the Great Martyr Prince Lazar. After many destructions and restorations, Podlastva thus continued its centuries-old role as one of Grbalj’s most important spiritual and historic centres.
