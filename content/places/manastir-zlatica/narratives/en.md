Zlatica Monastery stands at the Crkvine site in Podglavica, a hamlet of Doljani near Podgorica. It is an early Christian complex with the remains of two churches, a baptistery, an episcopal sarcophagus and a necropolis.

The complex was long dated to the sixth or early seventh century, while some art historians place the triconch church as early as the fourth century on the basis of the baptistery and the site’s episcopal character. A ninth-century restoration is recorded, and there are also suggestions that the Bishop of Zeta, Ilarion, had his seat here in the thirteenth century before Miholjska Prevlaka. These early dates and identifications require further archaeological and historical research.

Folk tradition connects the name Zlatica with Saint Sava. The older name Ilovica is associated with local deposits of clay. The holy place flourished until the Ottoman conquests of the fifteenth century.

The basilica remains reveal a three-nave building with a spacious semicircular apse and narthex, together with side chambers, one used for burials. The neighbouring triconch church has a narthex, atrium and central octagon with horseshoe-shaped apses. The complex preserves fragments of columns and carved stone, an ornate sarcophagus discovered in 1953 and an insufficiently researched necropolis. A residence was being arranged in an older house at the site, but the restoration was not completed.
