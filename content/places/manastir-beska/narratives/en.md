Beška Monastery, also known as Brezovica, stands on the island of the same name in Lake Skadar, between Starčevo and Moračnik. The complex arose in the Balšić period and was an important centre of monastic, scribal and literary life in Zeta.

Đurađ II Stracimirović Balšić built the Church of Saint George, probably in the last quarter of the fourteenth century. His wife Jelena Balšić, daughter of Prince Lazar, built the Church of the Annunciation of the Most Holy Theotokos in 1439–1440, with chapels dedicated to Saint Gregory Palamas and Saint Mary of Egypt. The founder’s inscription above the door bears the year 1438. Nikon’s *Hexaemeron* was produced in the monastery scriptorium in 1439–1440, followed by the *Gorica Miscellany* in 1441–1442.

The monastery appears in Ottoman registers of 1570–1571 and 1582 and was still active in the early seventeenth century. The time of its later abandonment is not reliably established. King Nikola restored the Annunciation Church in the early twentieth century. Liturgical life resumed late in the twentieth century, and extensive work on the churches and residences began in 2002. The Annunciation Church was consecrated on 7 April 2006, when Jelena Balšić’s remains were placed in a stone reliquary.

The Church of Saint George has a triconch plan, a spacious narthex with a cross vault and a three-part bell gable. The monastery keeps the relics of Jelena Balšić and particles of the relics of several saints, including Saint John Chrysostom, Saint George, Saint Demetrius, Saint Peter of Cetinje and Saint Arsenije of Srem. Its older icons are especially valuable, among them an eighteenth-century icon of Saint Nicholas made after a Novgorod model.
