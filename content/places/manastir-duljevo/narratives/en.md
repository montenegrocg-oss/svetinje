Duljevo Monastery stands in Paštrovići beneath Goli Vrh, about 470 metres above sea level. According to Paštrovići tradition, Saint Stefan of Dečani built it and dedicated it to Saint Stephen the Archdeacon. The monastery was considered a dependency of Visoki Dečani and served as a coastal refuge for monks travelling towards Mount Athos and Jerusalem.

Many people from Paštrovići began their monastic path at Duljevo. Among them was Danilo Kažanegra, who died in 1820 and was called the second founder; tradition also associates the monastery with the tonsure of the future Patriarch Arsenije III Čarnojević. A school operated in the larger monastery building, while the Paštrovići assembly met in the complex to make decisions and reconcile brotherhoods. In 1694 Bishop Savatije Ljubibratić sent monks from Savina to Duljevo.

The monastery was severely damaged in Mahmud Pasha Bushati’s campaign of 1785, but the Paštrovići restored it the following year. It was devastated in the First World War and restored in 1924 through the efforts of Boris Kažanegra. It also suffered in the Second World War, when Protosyncellus Varnava Bućin and nuns Gordana Đonović and Stoja Bulajić were killed nearby. The 1979 earthquake again badly damaged the church and residences.

The complex includes the Church of Saint Stephen the Archdeacon, a chapel of Saint Stefan of Dečani, a residence and remains of older buildings. Two hermitages stand above the monastery; tradition associates one with Saint Sava’s stay before his final journey to the Holy Land.
