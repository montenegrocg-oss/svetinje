Praskvica Monastery stands above Miločer and Sveti Stefan in the heart of Paštrovići. Dedicated to Saint Nicholas, it was for centuries one of the region’s most important spiritual, cultural and political centres. Its name is associated with a nearby stream whose water, according to tradition, had a taste and scent reminiscent of a peach—*praskva*.

Folk tradition places the beginnings of the monastery in the eleventh century, in the time of Prince Vojislav of Zeta, but no written confirmation of such an early date survives. It is also linked with an early fourteenth-century charter of King Milutin, now lost but mentioned in later Paštrovići documents. The oldest reliably preserved document dates from 1413, when Balša III Balšić, ruler of Zeta, and his mother Jelena donated land for building the Church of Saint Nicholas.

During the medieval and early modern periods, Praskvica held a special place in the life of Paštrovići. Tribal representatives gathered here, important documents were kept, and the Paštrovići Bankada—the traditional court and assembly of the tribes—operated at the monastery. It was therefore not only a monastic centre but also a place where major decisions for the region were made.

The monastery suffered badly in the early nineteenth century under French rule in the Bay of Kotor. Following a Paštrovići revolt, the monastery was plundered in 1812, Saint Nicholas’s Church destroyed, and much of its valuable archive destroyed or removed. Restoration could begin only after the French departure in 1813 and continued for several decades, until the mid-nineteenth century.

The complex includes the churches of Saint Nicholas and the Holy Trinity, residences and other old monastery buildings. Praskvica was also an important centre of literacy and art, preserving valuable icons, liturgical objects, manuscripts and other witnesses to its long past.

The 1979 earthquake also damaged the monastery, after which the complex was restored. Its centuries-long history and deep connection with Paštrovići make Praskvica one of the most important Orthodox holy places on the Budva coast.
