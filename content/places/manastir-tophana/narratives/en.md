The Monastery of the Great Martyr Demetrius—Tophana stands on the island of the same name in south-western Lake Skadar, near the Albanian border. Different sources also call the island Tophala, Tophalma, Planiku and Topaona.

Tophana belongs to the oldest layer of Lake Skadar’s monastic holy places. Its main Church of Saint Demetrius has a characteristic trefoil, or triconch, plan. Its architectural features associate it with churches built between the ninth and twelfth centuries, and it is considered a possible model for later triconch churches on the lake’s islands.

A strong stone *pyrgos*, or monastery tower with a defensive role, stands beside the church. It controlled access to this part of the lake and, together with the towers at Prečista Krajinska and Moračnik, formed part of a system protecting monasteries and important routes in medieval Zeta. Tradition says the tower also held a cannon position, one interpretation of the name Tophana.

The monastery was probably destroyed in the late seventeenth century, at the time of the Great Migration of the Serbs and the destruction of other Lake Skadar monasteries. It remained abandoned for centuries, while the ruins of its church and tower testified to earlier monastic life.

Restoration began in recent times. On the Annunciation, 7 April 2014, monastic life and work on the ancient holy place resumed. On Saint Demetrius’s feast the following year, the Divine Liturgy was celebrated here again after almost four centuries.

Today the complex includes the Church of the Great Martyr Demetrius, the old tower, a chapel dedicated to the Most Holy Theotokos of Peć Krasnica and a residence. Remains of a circular structure, thought to have served as a belfry, survive on the rise above the main church.
