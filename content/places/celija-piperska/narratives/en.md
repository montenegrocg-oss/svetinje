Piperska Cell Monastery stands above the village of Gornji Crnci in Piperi, on a high plateau overlooking the Bjelopavlići Plain. Its church is dedicated to the Nativity of the Most Holy Theotokos.

The monastery’s foundation is associated with Saint Stephen of Piperi, a former abbot of Morača Monastery and a contemporary of Saint Basil of Ostrog. According to tradition, around 1637 he built a small church and a cell at Gornji Crnci, from which the monastery took its name. For a time, the holy place was a dependency of Ostrog Monastery. The relics of Saint Stephen have been kept here for more than three centuries; during periods of Ottoman danger, they were twice hidden in the Piperi hills.

Abbot Mihailo Petrović added the narthex in 1815, and Aleksije Vukotić built the bell tower, dome and right wing of the residence in 1823. The monastery was also restored in 1889. Petar II Petrović-Njegoš opened a primary school at the monastery, which became an important educational centre for Piperi and Bjelopavlići. The monastery had an archive, a library and a bindery; the school operated until about 1948.

The complex suffered during and after the Second World War. Conservation and restoration followed the 1979 earthquake, and a complete renewal was finished in 1994. Enclosed by a wall, the complex comprises a single-nave church with an apse, dome, side choirs and a later narthex, monastic residences and a bell tower above the entrance gate.
