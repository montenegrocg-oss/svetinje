Dajbabe Monastery, dedicated to the Dormition of the Most Holy Theotokos, stands on the slopes of Dajbabska Gora near Podgorica. It is an active men’s monastery of the Metropolitanate of Montenegro and the Littoral and one of Montenegro’s most unusual cave holy places. Venerable Simeon of Dajbabe called it the “underground home of the Queen of Heaven” because its main church was formed within a natural cave and dedicated to the Dormition.
According to records published by the Metropolitanate, the modern monastery began with a vision experienced in 1895 by Petko Ivezić, a young shepherd from Dajbabe. Church tradition relates that a saint appeared while Petko was tending sheep on Dajbabska Gora and told him that an old holy place lay there and that a monastery should be built. Petko told Hieromonk Simeon Popović, then serving at Ostrog Monastery. Simeon came to Dajbabe and began excavating and enlarging the cave with local people. The tradition of an earlier Christian cult site is not presented as an independently confirmed archaeological fact, and the record does not identify the saint in the tradition categorically. The monastery was founded by Hieromonk Simeon Popović, later Venerable Simeon of Dajbabe. Work on the cave church was completed in 1897, and it was consecrated on 22 December that year in honour of the Dormition of the Most Holy Theotokos.
Venerable Simeon spent the rest of his life at Dajbabe, serving the people and tending the monastery. The Dormition church was created by enlarging the natural cave in the form of a cross. Its irregular plan is approximately 21.5 metres long and averages about 2.5 metres in width.
Natural recesses and enlargements in the rock were used for different functions within the church. Side arms and small chapels extend from the main space, while the largest enlargement lies to the south at a slightly lower level.
A spacious portico with two bell towers was built before the entrance in 1908. The portico and residences form the main visible part of the complex, while most of the church lies underground. Venerable Simeon painted the cave interior, adapting his compositions to the uneven rock surfaces and irregular layout. The walls depict Christ, the Most Holy Theotokos, prophets, hermits, holy fathers and saints of the local Church tradition, including Saint Peter of Cetinje.
Alongside the relics of Venerable Simeon, the Metropolitanate’s official description mentions icons painted by the saint himself, an icon of the Jerusalem Mother of God and an old Russian epitaphios. The record does not present the century in which the epitaphios was made as a confirmed fact.
