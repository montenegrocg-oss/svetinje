Cetinje Monastery, dedicated to the Nativity of the Most Holy Theotokos, stands in the historic centre of Cetinje beneath Orlov Krš. Ivan Crnojević, ruler of Zeta, founded it in 1484, as Ottoman pressure was shifting Zeta’s political and spiritual centre inland from the Lake Skadar region. In the following year, Ivan issued the monastery’s founding charter and established the seat of the Metropolitanate of Zeta there.

The original monastery stood at Ćipur, close to the present complex. From its foundation it was not only a monastic and metropolitan centre, but also one of the principal places of spiritual and cultural life in Zeta. The Crnojević printing house is associated with this period. In the late fifteenth century it produced some of the most important early South Slavic books printed in Cyrillic, including the *Oktoih prvoglasnik* of 1494.

The old Cetinje Monastery was destroyed in 1692 during Suleiman Bushati’s campaign against Cetinje. In the early eighteenth century, Metropolitan Danilo Petrović rebuilt the monastery on a nearby site, probably occupied earlier by the Crnojević court. Parts of the old monastery were incorporated into the new building, including Ivan Crnojević’s founder’s inscription, capitals and the Crnojević coat of arms.

During the eighteenth and first half of the nineteenth century, Cetinje Monastery played an exceptionally important role in the history of Montenegro. The metropolitans of the Petrović-Njegoš dynasty were both spiritual leaders and holders of secular authority, making the monastery for a time the centre of ecclesiastical, political and state life. Saint Peter of Cetinje and Petar II Petrović-Njegoš hold a particularly important place in its history.

The monastery was repeatedly damaged, burned and rebuilt over the centuries, yet remained the seat of the Metropolitans of Cetinje. The development of education in Cetinje was also connected with it. A school operated at the monastery, and institutions that played a significant role in Montenegro’s educational and cultural life developed under its auspices during the nineteenth century.

The sacred treasures kept at Cetinje Monastery give it particular significance. The relics of Saint Peter of Cetinje rest in the monastery church, while the right hand of Saint John the Baptist and a particle of the True Cross are among its most important treasures. The monastery also has a rich treasury of old manuscripts, printed books, icons, liturgical objects, vestments and other valuables from different periods.

Its centuries-long role as a metropolitan seat, its connections with the Crnojević and Petrović-Njegoš dynasties, its early printing house and its rich spiritual and cultural heritage have made Cetinje Monastery one of Montenegro’s most important Orthodox monasteries and historic sites.
