The Monastery of Saint Nicholas — Gradište is situated on a hill above Buljarica, not far from Petrovac. Traces of earlier structures, burials and other remains found around the monastery indicate that the site had been used since antiquity.

According to tradition, the beginnings of the monastery date to the early 12th century, while the Church of Saint Nicholas is traditionally associated with the year 1116. The earliest known written reference to Gradište dates from 1305 and appears in a charter of King Milutin of the Nemanjić dynasty. Another charter from 1310 records a grant of property to the monastery.

Like the nearby Duljevo Monastery, Gradište was a dependency of Visoki Dečani Monastery. It was also closely connected with Ratac Monastery near Bar. Monks from Ratac occasionally sought refuge at Gradište, and after Ratac was destroyed its monastic community moved here.

More reliable information about Gradište becomes available from the early 17th century, when the main Church of Saint Nicholas was restored. In 1620 the renovated church was frescoed by the priest Strahinja of Budimlja, one of the notable painters of his period. Dionisije and Stevan Davidović are recorded as patrons of the restoration. The frescoes of the Church of Saint Nicholas remain among the monastery’s most important artistic treasures.

Gradište suffered repeated destruction over the centuries. One of the most severe attacks took place in 1785, when the forces of Mahmud Pasha Bushatli devastated the monastery while returning from a campaign against Montenegro. Because of its proximity to the former Ottoman frontier, the complex was once protected by a strong defensive wall with a tower and firing loopholes, of which only traces survive today.

The monastery also played an important educational role among the Paštrovići. A school operated here for many years, first in the monastery tower and later in one of the monastic buildings. It continued to function as a primary school until the Second World War. Gradište is also associated with Abbot Stefan Paštrović, who took part in the printing of Orthodox books in Venice at the end of the 16th century.

During the First World War, the Austrian authorities removed the monastery bells. Further damage followed during the Second World War, when the Church of Saint Sava was struck by artillery shells and the monastery buildings were later burned.

The monastery was restored in 1972, when the residential buildings were rebuilt and damage to the churches was repaired. Only a few years later, the devastating earthquake of 1979 caused serious destruction once again. The vault of the Church of Saint Nicholas collapsed, resulting in the permanent loss of part of its historic fresco decoration. A major restoration of the complex followed.

Today the monastery consists of three churches. The principal church is dedicated to Saint Nicholas, another to Saint Sava, while the Church of the Dormition of the Most Holy Theotokos stands on a higher terrace. Its centuries-long history, surviving frescoes and close connection with the spiritual life of the Paštrovići make Gradište one of the most important Orthodox sanctuaries on this part of the Montenegrin coast.
