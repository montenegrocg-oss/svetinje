The Church of the Great Martyr Kyriake stands at Pelinovo in Upper Grbalj. According to old records cited by the Metropolitanate of Montenegro and the Littoral, a monastery dedicated to Saint Kyriake existed here in the Middle Ages. It is associated with the Nemanjić era and was connected with the Monastery of the Holy Archangel Michael at Miholjska Prevlaka.

The old monastery was eventually destroyed, and the present church was built in the sixteenth century. Tradition associates its construction with a member of the Ljepavić family. The church was extended in later centuries and consequently has an elongated rectangular plan.

Remains of older buildings around the church testify to the former importance of the site. Earthworks in the churchyard in 2001 uncovered substantial sections of a herringbone-patterned brick floor, together with fragments of pottery and amphorae. Old records also mention traces of mosaics, foundation walls and other remains of the former monastery complex.

The church has an iconostasis belonging to the well-known Dimitrijević–Rafailović icon-painting tradition of the Bay of Kotor. The icon of the Most Holy Theotokos preserves the signature of the icon painter Hristifor Rafailović and the date 1844.

The church was badly damaged in the great earthquake of 1979. Following restoration, it was reconsecrated in 1992 and resumed its role in the liturgical and communal life of Upper Grbalj.

A memorial to local people executed at this site during the Second World War stands close to the church. Alongside its spiritual importance, the Church of Saint Kyriake preserves many traces of the centuries-long history of Pelinovo and Grbalj.
