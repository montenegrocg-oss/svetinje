The Monastery of Saint Nicholas, also known as Orahovo Monastery, stands above the Orahovo Plain near Virpazar, at the foot of Mount Bjelasica and overlooking Lake Skadar.

According to tradition, the spiritual history of this place reaches deep into the Middle Ages. The older church, dedicated to Saint John the Baptist and popularly known as Boljan’s Church, is considered the oldest building in the complex. Its precise date is not reliably established: tradition associates it with the tenth century, while some researchers place it later.

Monastery tradition connects the Church of Saint Nicholas with the time of Saint Sava. According to tradition, Bishop Ilarion of Zeta built a new monastery church and associated buildings in 1233 with Saint Sava’s blessing. Orahovo later continued as a monastic centre under Ottoman rule, although it suffered repeatedly.

The monastery was probably destroyed in the late sixteenth century and restored in the seventeenth. The 1663 frescoes in the Church of Saint Nicholas are significant evidence of that renewal; they were painted in the time of Metropolitan Rufim Boljević and Abbot Vasilije Njeguš. The surviving frescoes show that Orahovo was an active monastic centre in the second half of the seventeenth century.

The monastery retained importance in Crmnica during the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries but eventually lost its brotherhood. In 1909 parts of the Church of Saint Nicholas were restored, a bell tower erected and a new iconostasis made.

The complex was damaged in the great earthquake of 1979. Modern restoration began in 2002, renewing the church, residence and approach road. The restored complex was consecrated in 2019.

Today Orahovo Monastery comprises two stone churches and a residence. The older church is dedicated to Saint John the Baptist and the main monastery church to Saint Nicholas. Saint Nicholas’s is a single-nave building with a semicircular apse and tall bell gable; valuable seventeenth-century frescoes adorn its interior.
