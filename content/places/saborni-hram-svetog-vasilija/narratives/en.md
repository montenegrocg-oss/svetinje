The Cathedral Church of Saint Basil of Ostrog stands on Petrova Glavica in Nikšić. It was built at the end of the 19th century in honour of Saint Basil of Ostrog and in memory of the Montenegrins and Herzegovinians who lost their lives in the wars of liberation of 1876–1878.
The initiative for its construction is associated with Prince Nikola I Petrović-Njegoš. The project received significant support from Imperial Russia and the Romanov dynasty, as well as contributions from numerous benefactors. The names of fallen soldiers are inscribed inside the church, giving the building a strong memorial character.
The church was completed and consecrated in 1900. Its prominent position on a hill above the city and its monumental appearance soon made it one of the most recognizable landmarks of Nikšić.
The construction of the Cathedral Church was also connected with the transfer of the ecclesiastical centre from Ostrog to Nikšić. From 1878, Ostrog Monastery served as the seat of the Eparchy of Zahumlje and Raška, before the centre was later transferred to Nikšić.
During the 20th century, the church underwent several restorations. Major works were carried out in the 1930s and again in 1989. In 2000, marking the centenary of its consecration, another extensive restoration was completed. That same year, the Eparchy of Budimlja and Nikšić was restored, and the Cathedral Church became one of its important spiritual and administrative centres.
The church is dedicated to Saint Basil of Ostrog, who is also venerated as the heavenly protector of Nikšić. On his feast day, 12 May, large liturgical gatherings take place here, together with the Saint Basil procession through the streets of the city, which has become one of the most important church events in Nikšić.
The Cathedral Church of Saint Basil of Ostrog therefore serves at once as a place of worship, a memorial to those who fought for liberation, and one of the most important monuments to the spiritual and historical heritage of Nikšić.
