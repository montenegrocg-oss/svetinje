The Monastery of the Holy Archangel Michael stands at Miholjska Prevlaka near Tivat. Archaeological research shows a multi-layered site with traces of life from the Roman period. An early Byzantine church of the sixth century stood on the site of the present complex, while in the ninth or tenth century the area was adapted for a Benedictine monastery.

Around 1220, in Saint Sava’s time and after the Serbian Church gained autocephaly, the old church complex was restored and the seat of the Diocese of Zeta established at Prevlaka. Over subsequent centuries, the monastery became one of Zeta’s most important spiritual centres. In the mid-fourteenth century under Emperor Dušan, the diocese was raised to a metropolitanate, giving the monastery particular importance in the Church life of the coast.

The monastery suffered heavily in the mid-fifteenth century, amid political and ecclesiastical conflict on the coast, after which monastic life at Prevlaka ended. According to Church tradition, many Prevlaka monks were poisoned and the Church of the Holy Archangel Michael destroyed. The remains of the main medieval church are still visible within the complex.

A new effort to restore Prevlaka began in the nineteenth century through Countess Ekaterina Vlastelinović. She purchased part of the island and began renewing monastery buildings. Lacking the funds to rebuild the large medieval church, she erected a smaller Church of the Holy Trinity. Its foundations were consecrated in 1832 and the completed church in 1833.

Research and restoration continued in the twentieth and early twenty-first centuries. Systematic archaeological excavations took place in 1956–1957 and again from 1997, when much of the old church remains was studied. Monastic life at Miholjska Prevlaka was restored in the late twentieth century. The monastery now lives again as an Orthodox community and as a place where one of the most important medieval Church centres of the Bay of Kotor and Zeta is being renewed.
