Obod Monastery stands on Obod hill above Rijeka Crnojevića, near the river’s source. In 1475 Ivan Crnojević built a monastery here with a Church of Saint Nicholas, which served for a time as the seat of the Metropolitans of Zeta.

An Ottoman register of 1485 mentions the “monastery of Ivan’s church”, but its identification with Obod Monastery is not entirely certain. Tradition also remembers two popular assemblies held by Jelena Balšić before the church. The monastery was destroyed in 1692 and a mosque built on its site. After Ottoman rule receded, the Church of Saint Nicholas was rebuilt in 1743. Architectural remains and a fifteenth-century grave indicate that parts of the older church may have survived.

The monastery was abandoned in the late eighteenth century and the church served as a parish church. Monastic life was renewed in 2000 with the restoration of the churches of Saint Nicholas and the Ascension, and the House of Saint Peter of Cetinje. Today the complex includes these churches, a chapel of Saint Peter of Cetinje, a hermitage of Saint Seraphim of Sarov according to the source, new residences and remains of older buildings.

The Obod printing house, an early centre of South Slavic Cyrillic printing, is associated with the monastery. Đurađ Crnojević obtained printing equipment in Venice, and Hieromonk Makarije printed the *Oktoih* in 1493–1494. The courtyard preserves remains of a building traditionally linked with the printing house and a memorial plaque recording its work.
