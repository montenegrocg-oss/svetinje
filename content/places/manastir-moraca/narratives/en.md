Morača Monastery stands in the valley of the River Morača, in the municipality of Kolašin. Dedicated to the Dormition of the Most Holy Theotokos, it is one of Montenegro’s most important medieval Orthodox holy places.

Prince Stefan, son of Vukan Nemanjić and grandson of Stefan Nemanja, built the monastery in 1251/1252. The main monastery church was soon decorated with frescoes, around 1260. The best-preserved scenes from this earliest layer are in the diaconicon, notably the cycle dedicated to the Prophet Elijah.

The monastery suffered severe damage in an Ottoman raid in the early sixteenth century. The lead roof was removed, the roof structure burned, and much of the original painting was destroyed during the period of abandonment. Restoration began after the renewal of the Patriarchate of Peć. In 1574, the church was restored and repainted through the efforts of Abbot Toma and Prince Vučić Vučetić of Rovca.

During the seventeenth century, Morača again became an important spiritual and artistic centre. Distinguished fresco and icon painters worked here, and over several generations its churches received new frescoes and icons. The monastery also played an important role in the life of the Patriarchate of Peć, whose patriarchs visited and supported it.

In the eighteenth and early nineteenth centuries, the monastery was an important gathering place for the people of Morača and neighbouring tribes during the struggles against the Ottoman Empire. In 1820 the Morača region became part of free Montenegro.

The fresco painting is a particular treasure of the monastery. Alongside rare surviving thirteenth-century frescoes, the church holds important works from the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, created during its great renewal. Its combination of Nemanjić architecture, medieval frescoes and later artistic layers makes Morača Monastery one of the most important sites of Orthodox spiritual and cultural heritage in Montenegro.
