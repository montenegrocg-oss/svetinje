The Monastery of the Dormition of the Most Holy Theotokos—Podmaine stands on a gentle rise above the Budva Plain, about two kilometres north of Budva’s Old Town. Historical sources and tradition also call it Maine and Podostrog, after the nearby Ostrog hill at Maine. The monastery was once an important spiritual centre and gathering place for the Maine tribe.

According to folk tradition, the monastery dates from the Crnojević period. Its earliest reliable record is from 1630, when Hieromonk Visarion of Cetinje Monastery renewed the frescoes of the small Church of the Entry of the Most Holy Theotokos into the Temple. Its architectural features suggest that the church may have originated in the fifteenth century.

During the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, Podmaine was closely associated with the Metropolitans of Cetinje and the Petrović-Njegoš dynasty. For a time, it served as a metropolitan residence. Metropolitan Danilo I Petrović-Njegoš reposed at Podmaine in 1735 and was buried here; his remains rested in the monastery until their transfer to Cetinje in 1856.

Metropolitan Sava Petrović-Njegoš built the second, larger church in 1747 and dedicated it to the Dormition. Rafail Dimitrijević of Risan painted its frescoes that year. The monastery also played an important role in the social life of neighbouring tribes, whose leaders held assemblies and made important decisions here.

Petar II Petrović-Njegoš was also connected with the monastery. The large stone terrace and cistern before the old residence are attributed to his time. In the late 1830s, as Austrian authority expanded along the coast, Podmaine passed under Austrian administration and monastic life gradually died out. An Austrian garrison and prison occupied the complex during the First World War.

The monastery declined over subsequent decades and was further damaged by the destructive 1979 earthquake. Renewal of the holy place and monastic life began in the late twentieth century. The old residence, terrace, bell tower and mill were restored, and a new residence was built on old foundations in the early twenty-first century. The whole complex was renewed by 2007.

Podmaine now comprises two churches—the older Church of the Entry of the Most Holy Theotokos and the larger Dormition Church—together with residences and other buildings. Architecture from different periods and centuries of connection with the Metropolitans of Cetinje and the Petrović-Njegoš dynasty make it an important historic holy place of the Budva region.
