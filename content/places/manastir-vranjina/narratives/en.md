The Monastery of Saint Nicholas on Vranjina stands on Lake Skadar near the mouth of the Morača. According to monastery tradition, it was founded between 1221 and 1233 with Saint Sava’s blessing by Ilarion, the first Bishop of Zeta and Saint Sava’s disciple. Built on the site of an older Byzantine church, it held an important spiritual and economic position during the Middle Ages.

Rulers of the Nemanjić, Balšić and Crnojević dynasties endowed Vranjina, and several preserved medieval documents testify to its past. By Emperor Dušan’s charter of 1348 it became a dependency of the Monastery of the Holy Archangels in Jerusalem. Elder Nikon of Jerusalem, a noted medieval spiritual writer, was also connected with Vranjina. For a period, it served as the seat of the Metropolitan of Zeta.

At its height, the monastery had three churches, extensive estates and service buildings. The main church was dedicated to the Translation of the Relics of Saint Nicholas, while the other two were reportedly dedicated to the Most Holy Theotokos and Saint Sava.

The monastery suffered repeatedly under Ottoman rule. Major destruction is recorded in 1482, 1714, 1843 and 1862, after which it never fully recovered its former medieval appearance and importance.

In 1886, Prince Nikola I, with the help of Russian Emperor Alexander III Romanov, built a new church and large residence. Monastic life gradually faded after the Second World War. In the early 1970s the large residence burned down, and many icons and other valuable objects were lost.

Restoration began on 1 August 1998, and Metropolitan Amfilohije consecrated the newly built small residence on 5 September that year. Monastic life was thereby renewed at this ancient holy place. Today the complex comprises Saint Nicholas’s Church, the old large residence under restoration, a small residence and a service building.
