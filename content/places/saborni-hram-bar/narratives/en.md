The Cathedral of Saint John Vladimir stands in central Bar and is dedicated to Saint John Vladimir, the medieval ruler of Duklja and one of the region’s most venerated saints. Built as the city’s principal Orthodox church, it is one of Montenegro’s most monumental modern ecclesiastical buildings.

The idea of building a large Orthodox church in the newer part of Bar arose after the 1979 earthquake. The long effort to obtain land gained new momentum in 1991, when a building committee was established with the blessing of Metropolitan Amfilohije of Montenegro and the Littoral. A central site was secured that year, where a cross was first erected and blessed.

Construction began in 2006 from a concept by architect Predrag Ristić, with architect Svetislav Popović responsible for the working design. A more intensive phase began in 2009, when the foundation slab was poured and the crypt built. Dedicated to Saint Alexander Nevsky, the crypt soon came into liturgical use.

The main structural work was completed in 2012 when a large cross modelled on the Cross of Saint John Vladimir was placed on the central dome. The church is about 54 metres long and nearly 37 metres wide, with a height of around 44.5 metres to the top of the cross.

Extensive frescoes executed in a traditional technique adorn the interior. The monumental image of the Mother of God “More Spacious than the Heavens” in the sanctuary apse is especially prominent. The iconostasis, altar table, thrones, floors and numerous architectural details use different kinds of stone. Bells cast in Russia hang in two towers dedicated to Saints Constantine and Helen and Saints Cyril and Methodius.

The cathedral was solemnly consecrated on 25 September 2016, the year marking a millennium since Saint John Vladimir’s martyrdom. Patriarch Theophilos III of Jerusalem presided over the consecration and Divine Liturgy, concelebrating with Serbian Patriarch Irinej, Archbishop Anastasios of Albania, Archbishop Jovan of Ohrid and many hierarchs and clergy of the local Orthodox Churches.

Particles of the relics of Saint Sava the Sanctified, Saint John Vladimir and the Martyrs of Jasenovac were placed in the altar at the consecration. The cathedral also received a particle of Saint John Vladimir’s relics, further underlining its dedication to the saint whose name is deeply connected with the history of Bar and the Lake Skadar region.

Today the Cathedral of Saint John Vladimir is a defining landmark of modern Bar and an important centre of Orthodox spiritual life on the Montenegrin coast.
