Rustovo Monastery stands in a wooded hollow of Paštrovska Gora, about 370 metres above sea level and above Sveti Stefan. Its oldest church, dedicated to the Dormition of the Most Holy Theotokos, dates from the fourteenth century.

Tradition connects the church’s construction with the memory of people from Paštrovići who died beyond Goli Vrh, but the number of victims and circumstances of their burial have not been independently established. Monastery tradition also tells of erosion that damaged the church in the fifteenth or sixteenth century. The first recorded restoration was carried out in 1683 with donations from people of Paštrovići living in Constantinople.

The church was badly damaged in the 1979 earthquake, and another restoration began in the 1990s. In the early twenty-first century the grounds were improved and a residence built. Its consecration on 18 January 2004 marked the renewal of monastic life at Rustovo.

The courtyard contains churches of the Dormition and Entry into the Temple of the Most Holy Theotokos and of the Holy Royal Martyrs, the Romanovs; the residence has a chapel of Saint Benedict of Nursia. The monastery also includes the Skete of Saint Spyridon on Paštrovska Gora. Older icons include the Tikhvin Icon of the Mother of God, an icon of Saint Gerasimos, and icons of Christ Pantocrator and Saint Nicholas. Monastery tradition particularly venerates the Iveron Icon and the icon of the Mother of God “Sweet-Scented Flower”, while reports of their streaming myrrh and working miracles belong to living Church tradition.
