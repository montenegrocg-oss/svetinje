The Monastery of the Dormition of the Most Holy Theotokos—Reževići stands above the Adriatic Sea between Sveti Stefan and Petrovac. It belongs to the old monasteries of Paštrovići that form the so-called Holy Mountain of Paštrovići. Its name comes from the nearby River Režević.

The monastery’s exact date is not reliably established. Folk tradition says that Stefan the First-Crowned built a small Church of the Dormition here, while a second church dedicated to Saint Stephen is associated with Emperor Dušan. Researchers note, however, that further archaeological study is needed to date the monastery’s earliest phases more precisely.

In the mid-sixteenth century, pirates from Ulcinj plundered the monastery churches and guest house, leaving the complex abandoned for a long period. A substantial renewal began in 1714 with the arrival of Prohegumen Maksim Kosijerevac from Kosijerevo Monastery. He restored the neglected churches and monastery building and, in agreement with the people of Reževići, renewed monastic life.

Archimandrite Nikodim Vuković continued his work and built a larger Church of the Holy Trinity in 1770. Hieromonk Dimitrije Perazić completed it in 1814. Perazić was particularly important in the monastery’s further renewal and in collecting aid in Russia; Russian donations funded the monastery bell tower built in 1839.

The old Church of Saint Stephen was destroyed in the late eighteenth century, but part of its walls and old frescoes survive within the later residence. These remains and other layers of frescoes are important evidence of earlier phases in the life of Reževići.

The monastery was repeatedly damaged, restored and extended over the centuries. It also suffered significantly in the 1979 earthquake, followed by conservation and restoration. Walls, vaults and roofs have been renewed in recent times with an effort to preserve the complex’s historic appearance.

Today Reževići Monastery is one of the most important spiritual and architectural centres of Paštrovići. Its layered past, remains of earlier churches and frescoes, and its eighteenth–nineteenth-century Holy Trinity Church testify to the long continuity of Orthodox monastic life on this part of the Montenegrin coast.
