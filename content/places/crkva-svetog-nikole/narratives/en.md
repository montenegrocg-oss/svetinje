The Church of Saint Nicholas stands in the northern part of Kotor’s Old Town, immediately beside the city walls. An earlier Orthodox church dedicated to Saint Nicholas had occupied the same site since 1810.

The older church was badly damaged by a fire on Christmas Eve in 1896. The fire destroyed much of its interior, including numerous icons and ecclesiastical treasures. Preparations were then made to build a new and considerably larger church.

Construction of the present church began in 1902 and continued until 1909. It was designed by the architect Ćiril Iveković in the Neo-Byzantine style. The monumental single-nave building has two tall bell towers on its principal façade, which have become a recognisable feature of old Kotor.

The richly decorated iconostasis by Franjo Cigler, an art teacher at Kotor Gymnasium, is a particular treasure of the interior. The principal icons have silver revetments made by Kotor craftsmen, and the church also keeps an important collection of old icons.

Its best-known sacred treasures include the Three-Handed Icon of the Most Holy Theotokos and a particle of the relics of Saint Nicholas the Wonderworker of Myra. Among the objects that survived the fire in the old church, a gold cross from the time of the Russian Empress Catherine the Great is especially notable. Petar II Petrović-Njegoš presented it to the church in Kotor.

The treasury, library and archive of the Serbian Orthodox Church in Kotor are housed at the Church of Saint Nicholas. They preserve icons, old books and documents, liturgical objects, vestments and other testimony to the centuries-long Orthodox life of the Bay of Kotor.

Today the Church of Saint Nicholas holds a central place in Kotor’s Orthodox life and is one of the most important sacred buildings in the historic heart of the city.
