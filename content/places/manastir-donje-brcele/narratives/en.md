Saint Nicholas Monastery is situated in the village of Donji Brčeli in Crmnica, not far from Virpazar. The exact date of its foundation has not been firmly established. It is most commonly dated to the 14th or 15th century, while different local traditions associate its origins either with Jelena Balšić or with Queen Helen of Anjou and the earlier 13th century.

According to the tradition concerning Jelena Balšić, daughter of Prince Lazar, the Church of Saint Nicholas was built in the early 15th century. Older records, however, preserve another tradition according to which the monastery was founded by Helen of Anjou, wife of King Uroš I, while members of the Balšić dynasty later used it as a summer residence. Because of these differing traditions, the earliest history of the monastery remains uncertain.

Donji Brčeli occupied an important place in the life of the Crmnica region. The Church of Saint Nicholas was regarded as the principal assembly church of Crmnica, while the monastery served as a centre of spiritual, educational and communal life. Monks taught young men preparing for the priesthood, and during the 19th century a regular school also operated at the monastery.

An especially important chapter in the monastery’s history is connected with Šćepan Mali, who ruled Montenegro from 1767 to 1773. He lived at Donji Brčeli for a period of time and conducted part of his governmental affairs from the monastery. Historical sources record that he was murdered there in 1773 and subsequently buried at the monastery, making Donji Brčeli one of the places most directly associated with his rule.

The Church of Saint Nicholas underwent a major reconstruction in 1861 during the reign of Prince Nikola I Petrović-Njegoš. Parts of the church were extended and a new bell gable was erected. Further restoration followed in 1891, when the sanctuary and bell structure were repaired.

The present church is an elongated single-nave stone building with a tall apse and a three-part bell gable. A marble plaque on the western façade bears the coat of arms and monogram of Prince Nikola together with the date of the 1861 restoration. Inside is a large wooden iconostasis containing 47 icons, while the present iconostasis is associated with the work of Vasilije Đinovski from the beginning of the 20th century.

The monastic buildings suffered severe damage during the Second World War, followed by further destruction in the 1979 earthquake. During restoration works after the earthquake, foundations of an older church were discovered beneath the floor of the present building, confirming that an earlier sanctuary had stood on the site.

On the night of 16–17 December 2023, a fire completely destroyed the monastery residence housed in the former school building. With the support of the faithful and benefactors, the building was reconstructed and the restored residence was consecrated on 21 December 2024.

With its long and layered history, Saint Nicholas Monastery at Donji Brčeli remains one of the important Orthodox sanctuaries of Crmnica, bringing together medieval traditions, the period of Šćepan Mali and the educational heritage of the region.
