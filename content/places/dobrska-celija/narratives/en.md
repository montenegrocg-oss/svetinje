Dobrska Cell Monastery stands at Dobrsko Selo near Cetinje. Its earlier dedication was to the Dormition of the Most Holy Theotokos, while the monastery now celebrates the Entry of the Theotokos into the Temple. Its name recalls its beginnings as a hermit’s cell.

The exact date of foundation is unknown. The holy place is thought to have originated in the fifteenth century, in the time of Ivan Crnojević, and to have been a dependency of Cetinje Monastery by the end of that century. Metropolitan Sava I stayed at Dobrska Cell after the old Cetinje Monastery was destroyed in 1692, temporarily making the complex a metropolitan residence. Later Metropolitans of Cetinje also spent winters here because of the mild climate.

The monastery’s educational role was particularly marked in 1842, when Petar II Petrović-Njegoš founded a village school here. With interruptions, it operated until 1912.

The single-nave church has a semicircular apse, a bell gable and a spacious wooden portico. A barrel vault covers the interior and the floor is laid with stone slabs. The residences, with a row of stone arches, are a valuable example of vernacular building within a sacred complex; a building used for the school was later added to the older wing.
