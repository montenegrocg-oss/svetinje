The Monastery of the Dormition of the Most Holy Theotokos—Savina stands in Herceg Novi on a rise surrounded by dense Mediterranean vegetation, close to the entrance of the Bay of Kotor. It is one of the Bay’s most important Orthodox spiritual and cultural centres.

The complex comprises three churches: the small and large churches of the Dormition and the Church of Saint Sava on the hill above. According to information preserved by monastery tradition, the small Dormition Church dates from the eleventh century. It was restored repeatedly, including substantial work in 1831 when an older layer of frescoes was also renewed.

A particularly important period began in the late seventeenth century. After Tvrdoš Monastery was destroyed, monks from Tvrdoš came to Savina in 1694 with Metropolitan Savatije Ljubibratić. They brought liturgical books, manuscripts and other treasures from Tvrdoš and Mileševa, making Savina an important guardian of Herzegovina’s spiritual and cultural heritage.

The monastery flourished during the eighteenth century. As Orthodox people arrived from Herzegovina, the existing churches became too small and preparations began for a large Dormition Church. Donations were collected throughout the Orthodox world, with substantial help from Serbs living and serving in Russia.

Construction began in 1777 after permission was obtained from the Venetian authorities and ended in 1799. The principal builder was Nikola Foregić of Korčula. Carefully dressed stone forms a monumental single-nave church with a spacious apse, dome and tall three-storey bell tower.

The interior is adorned with a richly carved and gilded iconostasis made in the late eighteenth century by Simeon Lazović and his son Aleksije. The treasury also holds an important collection of old manuscripts, charters, liturgical objects and icons from different periods.

The Church of Saint Sava stands on the hill above the monastery. Archaeological research places its origin in the late twelfth or early thirteenth century. An old tradition of Saint Sava’s stay in the Bay of Kotor is associated with it and gave the surrounding area the name Savina. The church suffered under Ottoman rule and was later restored.

Throughout its long history, Savina was not only a monastic centre but also an important centre of literacy, art and spiritual life for the Orthodox population of the Bay of Kotor. Its layered architecture, rich frescoes and treasury, and close connection with Tvrdoš and Herzegovinian monasticism give it a special place in the Orthodox heritage of the Montenegrin coast.
