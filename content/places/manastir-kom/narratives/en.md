Kom Monastery, dedicated to the Dormition of the Most Holy Theotokos, stands near Žabljak Crnojevića on a spur of Andrijska Gora. During periods of high water the site becomes an island, while in the dry season it can be reached across the surrounding marshland.

The monastery was built between 1415 and 1427 as an endowment of Đurađ and Lješ, or Aleksa, Crnojević. Kom held an important place in the ecclesiastical history of Zeta, as it served for a time as the seat of the Metropolitanate of Zeta. It also functioned as a burial place for the Crnojević family, and several members of the dynasty are buried inside the church.

The main church is a simple single-nave building dedicated to the Dormition of the Most Holy Theotokos. Side chapels and a spacious wooden porch were added around the middle of the 15th century. Its surviving frescoes are among the monastery’s greatest treasures: the church was first painted around 1470, while a second layer of frescoes was added in the second half of the 16th century.

During the 15th century, Kom possessed considerable estates. After Žabljak Crnojevića fell under Ottoman control in 1478, the monastery’s position became increasingly difficult. In 1485, when Ivan Crnojević founded Cetinje Monastery, he transferred to it properties that had previously belonged to Kom.

The monastery was active again around 1520, while the frescoes from the second half of the 16th century indicate that monastic and liturgical life continued. During the 17th and 18th centuries, however, Kom gradually fell into decline.

An important event in the monastery’s later history is associated with Petar II Petrović-Njegoš. On 31 January 1831, Metropolitan Zaharije of Raška and Prizren ordained the young Petar as a deacon and hieromonk at Kom and then elevated him to the rank of archimandrite.

Liturgical services were restored on the feast of the Dormition of the Most Holy Theotokos during the second half of the 19th century, when gatherings of people from the Ceklin region were also held at Kom. Nevertheless, the monastery remained largely abandoned throughout much of the 19th and 20th centuries.

Research and conservation work began in the 1980s, including structural repairs to the church and protection of its historic frescoes. A fuller revival of monastic life began in 1998, when monks returned to Kom and started restoring the monastic residences and other parts of the complex.

On 2 October 2005, Metropolitan Amfilohije consecrated the restored bell tower and part of the monastic residence, as well as a large cross erected on the hill above the monastery. Following its restoration, Kom once again became an active monastery and a place of pilgrimage on Lake Skadar.
