The Church of the Nativity of the Most Holy Theotokos at Ćipur, also known as the Court Church, stands in the historic centre of Cetinje beside King Nikola’s Palace. It was built on the site of Ivan Crnojević’s old Cetinje Monastery and, together with the monastery’s preserved foundations, is one of the most important places in the history of Cetinje and the Metropolitanate of Zeta.

In the late fifteenth century, after returning from exile, Ivan Crnojević, ruler of Zeta, moved his political and spiritual centre to the Cetinje Plain. He first built a court at Ćipur, followed in 1484 by a monastery dedicated to the Nativity of the Most Holy Theotokos. The seat of the Metropolitanate of Zeta was transferred there, making Cetinje the new spiritual centre of Zeta. Ivan Crnojević was buried in his endowment after his death in 1490.

The old monastery complex was considerably larger than the present church. At its centre stood the Church of the Nativity of the Most Holy Theotokos, surrounded by a portico with stone columns; residences, a tower and other monastic buildings stood around it. The monastery was destroyed in 1692 during fighting between Venetian and Ottoman forces at Cetinje and was not rebuilt on the same site. In the early eighteenth century, Metropolitan Danilo Petrović built the new Cetinje Monastery near the ruins.

Prince Nikola I Petrović-Njegoš built the present Court Church on the foundations of the earlier holy place in 1886, also providing a church for the religious needs of the Montenegrin court. The church is much smaller than the former monastery church, so the conserved foundations of Ivan Crnojević’s complex remain visible around it. Its interior is adorned with an iconostasis made by Russian icon painters in the late nineteenth century.

The graves of Montenegrin rulers give Ćipur particular importance. In 1989, the remains of King Nikola I and Queen Milena were brought from San Remo and buried in the church. Archaeological research in the late twentieth century also uncovered the remains of Ivan Crnojević, which were reinterred in a restored tomb in the church in 2012.

The Church at Ćipur thus brings together two important layers of Cetinje’s past: the remains of the Crnojević monastery, from which the city’s spiritual centre developed in the late fifteenth century, and the nineteenth-century court church of the Petrović-Njegoš dynasty. Services are still held here regularly, and the church bears the same dedication as Ivan Crnojević’s original endowment—the Nativity of the Most Holy Theotokos.
