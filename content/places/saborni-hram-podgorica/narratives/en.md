The Cathedral of the Resurrection of Christ stands in Podgorica’s Momišići neighbourhood. It is the city’s principal Orthodox church and one of Montenegro’s most important modern ecclesiastical buildings.

The idea of a large cathedral in Podgorica existed before the Second World War, but wartime and post-war circumstances prevented its realisation. A new initiative began in the late twentieth century, and Church land at Momišići, on the site of an old dependency of Hilandar Monastery, was chosen for construction.

Architect Predrag Ristić prepared the concept with the blessing of Metropolitan Amfilohije of Montenegro and the Littoral. Construction began in 1993. On 9 August that year, Ecumenical Patriarch Bartholomew and Serbian Patriarch Pavle solemnly laid the foundation stone in the presence of Metropolitan Amfilohije, many clergy and faithful.

The cathedral took twenty years to build. Although work was unfinished, liturgical life gradually began during construction. The Crypt of All Saints became a particularly important part of the complex, with regular Sunday and feast-day liturgies from 2008.

The architecture draws on medieval Orthodox building traditions, using natural stone and strongly emphasised monumentality. Its plan is an inscribed Greek cross with a large dome over the centre. Two towers at the western end contain chapels of Saint Simeon the Myrrh-streamer and Saint John Vladimir, together with a bell tower. The cathedral rises more than 40 metres to the cross on the central dome.

Rich interior decoration is a particular treasure. Almost the entire cathedral and crypt are covered with around 6,200 square metres of frescoes. Several painters worked on them from 2006 to 2013. The iconographic programme includes scenes from the lives of Christ and the Theotokos and depictions of saints, martyrs, hierarchs and figures important in Church history.

Seventeen bells cast in Russia hang at the cathedral, including a great bell weighing about eleven tonnes. A four-metre gilded cross, modelled on the Cross of Saint John Vladimir, crowns the central dome.

After twenty years of construction, the Cathedral of the Resurrection was solemnly consecrated on 7 October 2013, in the year marking 1,700 years since the Edict of Milan. Primates and representatives of several local Orthodox Churches attended, making it one of the most important Church events in Montenegro’s recent history.

Today the Cathedral of the Resurrection is the centre of Orthodox spiritual life in Podgorica and one of the modern city’s most recognisable landmarks.
