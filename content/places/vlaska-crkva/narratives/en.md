Vlaška Church stands in the historic centre of Cetinje and is dedicated to the Nativity of the Most Holy Theotokos. Its beginnings predate Ivan Crnojević’s arrival and the foundation of Cetinje Monastery.

According to folk tradition, the family of Ivan Borojević moved from Stari Vlah to the Cetinje Plain in the mid-fifteenth century. His sons are said to have built the first church of wattle, covered it with straw and dedicated it to the Nativity of the Most Holy Theotokos. The name Vlaška Church is traditionally associated with these settlers. The surviving archival sources do not allow the tradition to be fully confirmed or rejected.

The earliest certain written record dates from 1485, when Vlaška Church is mentioned by that name in Ivan Crnojević’s charter to Cetinje Monastery.

The church was repeatedly restored and changed in later centuries. It acquired its present form in 1864, when the people of Donji Kraj restored and enlarged it, as an inscription above the entrance records. It is a single-nave stone building with a sanctuary apse to the east and bell gable to the west.

An iconostasis made in 1878 by Vasilije Đinovski, an icon painter from Macedonia who refined his art in Russia, adorns the interior.

An old necropolis with many *stećci* once surrounded the church. Many were reused as building material during restorations, while some survive in the churchyard. Numerous important figures of Montenegrin history are buried in and around the church, including Metropolitans Ilarion Roganović and Visarion Ljubiša.

One of the church’s most striking features is the fence built in 1897 from captured rifle barrels taken in wars against the Ottoman Empire. Metal spear-shaped points cap the barrels, making the fence one of the church’s most recognisable details.

Vlaška Church is also connected with the Petrović-Njegoš dynasty. Prince Nikola Petrović-Njegoš and Milena Vukotić were married here in 1860, and a thanksgiving service for the royal couple was held in the church when Nikola I was proclaimed king in 1910.

Through its continuity from the earliest development of Cetinje to the present, Vlaška Church remains one of the most important witnesses to the city’s spiritual and historical past.
