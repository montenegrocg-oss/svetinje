Ždrebaonik Monastery stands on the left bank of the Zeta, on the Bjelopavlići Plain near Danilovgrad. No written information about its foundation survives. Research has shown that the present church rests on the foundations of a larger church dating from the late sixteenth or early seventeenth century.

Folk traditions associate its origin with the Nemanjić dynasty, but their historical confirmation remains unresolved. According to a later record, the Russian researcher Kovalevsky saw a charter at Morača in 1841 describing Ždrebaonik as a dependency of Morača; this claim requires further verification. The present church was restored in 1818, the bell tower built in 1926, and another restoration is recorded in 1885.

For centuries the monastery was a gathering place of the Bjelopavlići. It has been organised as a women’s monastery since 1991. A new residence with a chapel of Saint Arsenije was built between 1996 and 2000.

The Church of the Holy Archangel Michael is a single-nave building with a semicircular apse, an added narthex and a tall bell tower. The Đinovski brothers made the iconostasis in the early twentieth century, with a masonry structure decorated with stucco vines. Since 1920, the church has kept the relics of Saint Arsenije of Srem, transferred from Ostrog after the destruction of Kosijerevo in 1914. They were temporarily moved after the 1979 earthquake and returned to a restored reliquary in 1989.
