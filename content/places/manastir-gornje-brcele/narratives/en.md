Gornje Brčele Monastery stands in the Crmnica village of the same name. Its stone buildings are arranged on terraced ground and blend harmoniously with the traditional architecture of Crmnica. Folk tradition associates the beginnings of the holy place with the Balšić period, while Metropolitan Danilo I built the present Church of the Protection of the Most Holy Theotokos in the early eighteenth century.

The monastery served as the winter residence of the Metropolitans of Cetinje and is consequently also known as the Petrović Monastery. In Metropolitan Sava’s time, lightning ignited ammunition stored beneath the sanctuary and destroyed the church and monastery houses; Sava subsequently rebuilt the complex. At Prince Nikola’s order, a school for the Crmnica nahija opened at the monastery in 1860, and the whole complex was restored in 1906.

A stone wall encloses the Church of the Protection, the old and newer residential wings, the Brčele school and service buildings. The old wing is distinguished by Crmnica-style arches and a terrace, while a single bell gable stands beside the south gate.

Beside the church are the graves of Metropolitan Ruvim—whose numerical designation varies in the literature—and Metropolitan Arsenije Plamenac. The monastery was damaged in the 1979 earthquake; restoration was completed and the complex consecrated in 1993. The treasury preserves Russian liturgical objects and a silver reliquary bearing an inscription from 1731.
