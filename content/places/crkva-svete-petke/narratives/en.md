Maine and the Budva Plain have a long Orthodox and monastic tradition. Old records mention a monastery of Saint Petka that stood on the Maine Plain and held an important place among the holy sites of the Budva region. During the seventeenth century, Stanjevići Monastery was recorded as its dependency, and in 1630 Metropolitan Ruvim of Zeta used income from the Stanjevići estates to restore the Monastery of Saint Petka.

The ancient Monastery of Saint Mark the Apostle also stood close to the present church. According to early accounts, this monastery was older than Podmaine and, for a time after the destruction of the Monastery of the Holy Archangel Michael at Miholjska Prevlaka, served as the seat of the Metropolitanate of Zeta. Saint Mark’s Monastery was destroyed in the nineteenth century, and its foundations survive beside the present Church of Saint Petka.

In 2009, the beginning of restoration on the remains of the ancient Church of Saint Mark was blessed and a new foundation stone laid. The restored structure was conceived as a baptistery and part of the wider church complex of Saint Petka.

A covering from the relics of Venerable Mother Paraskeva is a particular treasure kept in the church. It was brought from Iași in Romania, where Saint Petka’s relics rest, and presented to the Budva church when the vestments on the saint’s relics were changed.
