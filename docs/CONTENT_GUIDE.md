# Svetinje.me Content Guide

## Purpose

This guide defines the editorial standard for public content about Orthodox monasteries, churches, and holy places in Montenegro. It applies to Serbian Cyrillic source content, Russian and English translations, structured facts, practical information, captions, metadata, and correction work.

Accuracy and respect take precedence over volume, speed, search traffic, and stylistic flourish.

## 1. Core editorial principles

Every public item must be:

- traceable to allowed sources;
- clearly separated from drafts and research notes;
- respectful of Orthodox faith, worship, clergy, monastic communities, pilgrims, and local communities;
- historically careful;
- written in a clear, modern, non-sensational style;
- explicit about uncertainty;
- reviewed at the level appropriate to its risk;
- current enough for its subject matter.

Never:

- invent a fact, date, location, coordinate, schedule, contact, quotation, tradition, or historical event;
- fill a missing field with a plausible value;
- turn an unverified legend into a factual statement;
- copy protected text or images without permission;
- publish machine translation without human review;
- present old practical information as current;
- use promotional exaggeration such as unique, miraculous, oldest, most important, or must-see unless the exact claim is supported and contextually appropriate.

An omitted fact is preferable to an unsupported one.

## 2. Content states and what they mean

### Research

Working material collected to investigate a subject. Research is not approved content and must never be rendered publicly. Restricted notes, copyrighted scans, personal contact details, and unpublished correspondence do not belong in the public repository.

### Draft

Prose or structured data being prepared. A draft may contain unresolved questions, but uncertainty must be explicit. Draft status does not imply factual verification.

### Fact review

The content is being checked against its cited sources. Each material claim must be supported, qualified, or removed.

### Language review

The facts have passed review, and the text is being checked for Serbian style or translation quality. Language review must not introduce new unsupported facts.

### Approved

Required factual and language reviews are complete. Approved content may appear in a protected preview.

### Published

The publishing reviewer has confirmed eligibility for production. Publication is a distinct action; file presence does not equal publication.

### Needs re-verification

A previously reviewed field or page requires another check. Stable historical prose may remain visible if safe, but time-sensitive fields follow the stricter stale-data rules below.

### Archived

The record remains in history but is not included in the active public catalogue.

## 3. Verification classes

Every meaningful factual field or claim should be understood as one of these classes:

### Verified

Supported by an allowed source, reviewed by a factual reviewer, and not contradicted by a stronger source. Verified does not mean permanently true; time-sensitive facts also need freshness metadata.

### Requires verification

Not yet sufficiently supported. It may remain in research notes or a draft marked clearly for editors, but it must not be presented publicly as fact.

### Disputed

Credible allowed sources disagree, or an authority challenges the current wording. Public use requires qualified language, citation of the differing positions when appropriate, and senior editorial approval.

### Unknown

No acceptable information is available. The field should be omitted rather than guessed.

### Not applicable

The field does not apply to this content type. Do not confuse this with unknown or missing.

## 4. Allowed sources

AGENTS.md permits:

- official church websites;
- diocesan websites;
- monastery websites;
- official publications;
- historical and academic sources.

The project charter also recognizes archival materials and scientific sources. Government or institutional geographic and heritage records may support administrative or geographic facts when they are official and relevant.

### Source priority

Use the strongest appropriate source for the claim:

1. Current official source directly responsible for the information.
2. Official church, diocesan, monastery, archival, heritage, or government publication.
3. Peer-reviewed or academically credible historical work.
4. Other official publications with clear authorship and provenance.

A source’s authority is claim-specific. A current monastery page may be strong for official contact information but not sufficient by itself for a contested medieval date. An academic work may be strong for historical analysis but unsuitable for today’s service schedule.

### Discovery-only material

Search results, travel listings, social posts, map pins, unsourced articles, copied directories, and AI-generated text may help locate an authoritative source. They are not sufficient publication sources by themselves.

Wikipedia may help discover references but is not an approved final source under the project rules unless the owner later changes the policy.

## 5. Source recording

Each source record should capture:

- stable source ID;
- title;
- publisher or institution;
- author when known and relevant;
- source type;
- URL or complete bibliographic reference;
- publication date when known;
- date accessed for online sources;
- original language;
- relevant page, chapter, section, or paragraph;
- source status;
- copyright and reuse notes.

For important historical claims, cite the source at paragraph or section level. A general bibliography at the bottom of a page is not enough when an editor cannot identify which source supports a claim.

If a source becomes unavailable, retain the record, mark its status, and seek a replacement. Do not silently remove provenance from published facts.

## 6. Serbian Cyrillic source content

Serbian Cyrillic is the primary editorial language and the reference for translation.

Serbian content should:

- use standard Serbian Cyrillic;
- preserve officially attested names and ecclesiastical terminology;
- use clear sentences and restrained vocabulary;
- distinguish an official name from a popular or historical alternate name;
- explain unfamiliar historical or ecclesiastical terms where useful;
- use consistent date, capitalization, and quotation conventions established by the Serbian language reviewer.

Do not transliterate source prose into Latin script as a substitute for Serbian Cyrillic. ASCII transliteration may be used later for approved URL slugs, but it does not replace visible Cyrillic content.

A terminology glossary must be established before broad translation work. Until then, uncertain terminology is flagged for language review rather than standardized by guesswork.

## 7. Russian and English translations

A translation is a separate editorial artifact linked to:

- the stable content entity;
- the approved Serbian source revision;
- its locale;
- translator;
- translation status;
- reviewer;
- translated and reviewed dates.

Translations must preserve meaning, degree of certainty, source references, and respectful tone. They must not add facts absent from the approved source unless those additions undergo factual review and are also reconciled with the source record.

Machine translation may be used only as drafting assistance. A qualified human must review the complete public result.

If no approved translation exists:

- do not publish Serbian text under the missing locale;
- do not create a thin placeholder page for search engines;
- offer a clear link to an available language when useful.

## 8. Names, dates, and historical claims

### Names

Record the preferred current name and any alternate names separately. Each alternate name requires context such as historical period, source language, or common usage. Do not merge distinct places because their names are similar.

### Dates

Use the level of precision supported by the source. Do not convert a century, approximate range, or traditional attribution into an exact year.

Qualifiers such as approximately, before, after, traditionally attributed, first recorded, rebuilt, or restored must remain attached to the claim they qualify.

Calendar conventions and feast dates require consistent editorial rules and appropriate context. These rules must be approved before a large body of content is entered.

### Historical disagreement

When allowed sources disagree:

- do not select the most dramatic version;
- identify the disagreement in editorial notes;
- prefer cautious public wording;
- cite the relevant positions when the disagreement is material;
- obtain factual-review approval.

### Tradition and legend

A tradition or legend may be culturally important, but label it accurately. Acceptable framing distinguishes phrases such as according to tradition from documented historical fact. Do not use a tradition to supply missing chronology or geography.

## 9. Time-sensitive information

Time-sensitive fields include:

- service schedules;
- visiting or opening hours;
- phone numbers;
- email addresses;
- official websites;
- seasonal access;
- road and transport conditions;
- accessibility arrangements;
- fees or permissions if ever applicable;
- temporary closures;
- event dates.

Every time-sensitive value requires:

- source IDs;
- verified-at date;
- verified-by identity;
- optional valid-from and valid-until dates;
- freshness status;
- display policy when stale.

Rules:

- Never infer a current schedule from a previous year.
- Never copy contact details from an unverified directory.
- If a current official source is unavailable, omit the value or state that visitors should verify through an approved official channel.
- When valid-until passes, the value becomes stale automatically in editorial terms even if no code enforces it yet.
- A stale schedule, closure, or contact must not continue to appear as current by default.
- A reviewer may allow a stable official website to remain visible while flagged for re-check, but schedules and temporary access conditions require stricter handling.

The project owner must approve concrete review intervals before launch. Until then, editors should set explicit valid-until dates whenever the source provides them and use needs re-verification conservatively.

## 10. Geographic information

A coordinate is a factual claim.

Record:

- latitude and longitude;
- WGS 84 reference;
- source;
- verified-at date;
- reviewer;
- accuracy class;
- whether publication is safe.

Do not increase coordinate precision beyond the source. A general area, settlement, complex centroid, and entrance are different accuracy levels.

A geocoder or map pin is a research suggestion, not final verification. Sensitive locations may be generalized or withheld.

Directions, parking, walking difficulty, route safety, and accessibility are separate claims and require their own support and dates.

## 11. Practical information

Separate practical information from historical prose so it can be reviewed and withdrawn without rewriting the history section.

Use direct, neutral wording. Distinguish:

- verified facility or access fact;
- editor observation with date;
- official instruction;
- recommendation;
- information still requiring verification.

Dress, conduct, photography, worship, and visitor guidance should come from an appropriate official source or a project-wide policy approved by the owner. Do not invent institution-specific rules.

## 12. Images and media

Use only:

- original project photography with documented rights;
- authorized material;
- material with a compatible license and correct attribution.

Each media record requires:

- creator;
- copyright owner;
- permission or license basis;
- required credit;
- allowed uses;
- publication status;
- related entities;
- localized alt text and caption;
- withdrawal or expiry details where applicable.

Alt text describes the meaningful visual content and purpose. Captions may add verified context but must not make unsupported claims.

Large originals do not belong in Git. Do not commit raw camera files, video, audio, 3D assets, or bulk archives.

## 13. Recommended place-page content order

When information is available and approved:

1. Official or preferred name and place type.
2. Short verified summary.
3. Hero media with credit.
4. Spiritual and historical context.
5. Architecture, art, relics, icons, or traditions where sourced.
6. Location and map.
7. Practical visiting information with freshness labels.
8. Accessibility information where verified.
9. Sources and last factual review.
10. Correction link.

Do not create empty sections or filler. A shorter verified page is acceptable.

## 14. SEO and metadata

SEO text is editorial content and follows the same accuracy rules.

- Titles and descriptions must reflect the visible page.
- Do not add unsupported superlatives or marketing language.
- Do not create pages solely to target keyword variants.
- Russian and English metadata must be reviewed translations.
- Structured data must not contain facts absent from the page.
- A page that is not eligible for publication is not eligible for indexing.

## 15. Pre-publication checklist

Before approval, confirm:

- the entity identity is unambiguous;
- all material factual claims have allowed sources;
- uncertainty and disagreement are labeled;
- dates use supported precision;
- time-sensitive fields have verification metadata;
- coordinates have source and accuracy;
- media rights and credit are complete;
- Serbian Cyrillic language review is complete;
- each translation points to the approved Serbian revision;
- no machine-generated text remains unreviewed;
- no copied text or image lacks permission;
- metadata matches visible content;
- corrections and source notes are preserved;
- publication status is explicit.

## 16. Open Phase 0 editorial decisions

The following still require owner or named-editor approval:

- exact Serbian, Russian, and English section vocabulary;
- URL transliteration and slug rules;
- house style for dates, feast dates, quotations, capitalization, and names;
- named factual, language, translation, and publishing reviewers;
- review intervals for each time-sensitive field type;
- minimum content required for a place to publish;
- correction response targets;
- treatment of sensitive geographic locations;
- approved analytics and privacy language.

These open decisions must not be filled by assumption.
